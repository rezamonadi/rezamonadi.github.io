How it works

p = qℓ   ·   F± = ±qE
τ = p × E   ·   U = −p · E

The dipole moment points from negative to positive. In a uniform field, the electric forces cancel, but their torques generally add.

Increasing q increases the forces. Increasing ℓ increases their lever arms. Both increase p and the maximum torque.

This rigid dipole has a fixed center and a fixed moment of inertia I, independent of q and ℓ, to isolate the effect of changing p. Charge-marker size is illustrative. Adjusting controls can add or remove energy.

Orientation versus time

Orientation from the rightward horizontal, wrapped to ±180°. A jump at ±180° represents continuous rotation.
Without damping, small oscillations satisfy T ≈ 2π√(I / p|E|). With I fixed, increasing p makes them faster.
